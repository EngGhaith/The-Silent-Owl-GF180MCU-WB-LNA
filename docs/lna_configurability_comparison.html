Six topologies, five bands: schematics, device operating points, and full performance

Updated comparison covering all 30 variants explored this session — dev_ilir and a from-scratch inductively-degenerated redesign of it, dev_ghaith and a from-scratch passive-output-matched redesign of it, main, and main with its noise-cancellation path removed (main_no_cancellation) — each retuned to its native 2.4 GHz plus GNSS L1 (1575.42 MHz), GNSS L2 (1227.60 MHz), LoRa EU868 (868.30 MHz) and LoRa EU433 (433.92 MHz). Includes each topology's schematic, transistor-level gm/Id, and S-parameter/noise/current results side by side at every band. The two new redesigns are not yet merged into the dev_ilir/dev_ghaith branch lines — they're tracked here purely as a side-by-side comparison against the existing implementations.

01

The six topologies

All six share the same GF180MCU 6V/3.3V device set and 50 Ω port convention. They differ in how the common-gate input stage is degenerated, whether there's a noise-cancellation path, and how the output is matched. 02

Retuning recipe: what to change, per branch, to move off 2.4 GHz

Each topology's native design point is 2.3–2.5 GHz. These are the exact, minimal component changes — nothing else in the schematic moves — needed to relocate each one to each target band. Every recipe below, old and new, changes only capacitors, resistors, and bias; no inductor, transistor size, or topology change is needed anywhere in this study. For the two passive-redesign variants (dev_ghaith passive output and dev_ilir inductive degen.), the inductors LD4/LD2/LDEG are deliberate parts of an R/L/C matching network fixed at their 2.4GHz values — band relocation instead lowers the CG-stage tank's damping resistor RLD3 and adds a new shunt cap CTUNE_M2 to pull the resonance down, retuning the output/input coupling (C11, C2, RLD2) to re-match.

Reading across all 30 variants

  • main wins or ties on S21 at every single band, by a wide margin below 2.4GHz — because its first stage was never resonant to begin with, it never suffered the gain collapse that forced dev_ilir and dev_ghaith into increasingly desperate shunt/series-cap rescues at the low end.
  • S11 was fixable the same way S22 was. main's input path has its own fixed series coupling cap (C2, the twin of C3) whose reactance also grows at low frequency. Enlarging it per band took S11 from a borderline -6 to -14dB range down to a consistently strong -15 to -24dB across all 5 bands, with S21 and NF improving as a free side effect at every single band.
  • LoRa EU433 is the dividing line. dev_ilir (−9.4dB) and dev_ghaith (−5.9dB) both fail to reach positive gain there no matter what was tried — shunt caps, series caps, every live bias pin. main reaches +12.1dB. That's the clearest evidence in this whole study that the resonant-first-stage design is a structural ceiling, not a tuning gap.
  • main costs more than double the current of dev_ghaith at every band (36.3mA vs. 15.1mA) and about 1.6× dev_ilir's (22.2mA) — and it's a materially more complex circuit (8 live transistors vs. 3–4). For GNSS L1/L2 and LoRa EU868, where all three topologies work, that's a real tradeoff against dev_ghaith's much lower power and simpler layout.
  • gm/Id tells the current story at the device level: dev_ghaith and main both run their CG input device (M2) in weak inversion (gm/Id ≈ 10–11/V) for maximum current efficiency; dev_ilir runs M2 in moderate inversion (gm/Id ≈ 4.4/V) even after its own PR #17 rebias — it is fundamentally less current-efficient at the input device, independent of any RF retuning.
  • The noise-cancellation path in main was never earning its keep. This session's own A/B proof (toggling the cancellation cap with everything else held fixed) measured NF_IMPROVEMENT_DB 0 at every frequency in the target band — the sense-and-subtract path (M7, M9, C7, C10, R6, R3) was not lowering NF at all, while still loading node vx and node s with its own parasitics and drawing current. Removing it outright (main_no_cancellation) and re-deriving the bias/cap operating point from scratch — rather than just starving the branch's current, which would leave the parasitics behind — improved every figure of merit at 2.4 GHz at once: S11 −20.8→−41.4dB, S21 17.5→18.4dB, S22 −12.6→−22.1dB, NF 3.10→2.98dB, for only +7.6% current (21.1→22.7mA) and 2 fewer transistors. The same cap-only relocation recipe that works for main carries over directly, and still reaches LoRa EU433 (+12.5dB) where dev_ilir/dev_ghaith cannot.
  • dev_ghaith didn't need a buffer removed — it needed its output taken seriously. It never had an active buffer; its output was always a single fixed 4pF cap riding on an incidentally-sized drain tank (−16.3dB S22). Retuning that same LD2/C11 pair as a deliberate L-match at 2.4GHz improved every band at once: 2.4GHz S21 17.7→21.6dB, S22 −16.3→−37.1dB. Band relocation itself uses only caps, resistors, and bias — LD2/LD4 stay fixed at their 2.4GHz values; instead, lowering the CG-stage tank's damping resistor RLD3 and adding a new shunt cap CTUNE_M2 pulls the resonance down: GNSS L1 S21 10.1→23.2dB, GNSS L2 and LoRa EU868 both land cleanly too. LoRa EU433 still fails, exactly as it did before (S21 −5.9→−7.7dB) — the CG-stage tank's gain crosses zero before the peak can reach 433.92MHz, a structural floor that caps/R/bias alone cannot cross.
  • dev_ilir's active current-sink was costing both power and match. Replacing M5 with a single degeneration inductor LDEG — the textbook inductively-degenerated CG technique — and rederiving VBIAS_CG from scratch (2.20→0.80V, since there's no current-source device left to set M2's point) dropped total current 21% (110.9→87.4mW) while S11 went from −17.7 to −35.3dB and S22 from −16.1 to −39.2dB at 2.4GHz. The same caps/resistors/bias-only band-relocation recipe as dev_ghaith (passive output) — lower RLD3, add CTUNE_M2 — carries over directly and reaches all three of GNSS L1 (S11 −15.0dB), GNSS L2, and LoRa EU868 (S11 −20.2dB) with LDEG/LD2/LD4 never touched. The one place it doesn't help: LoRa EU433 still fails, for the same structural reason as dev_ghaith — the CG-stage tank's gain goes negative before the peak reaches 433.92MHz.

Net recommendation, updated with both redesigns (not yet merged into the dev_ilir/dev_ghaith branch lines): if LoRa EU433 coverage is required, main_no_cancellation remains the only variant that reaches it — neither new redesign changes that, since relocating a band without retuning the inductors cannot move the CG-stage tank's gain-zero crossing past 433.92MHz for either topology. If EU433 is not required, the picture is straightforwardly better: the new inductively-degenerated dev_ilir and the new passive-output dev_ghaith are both strict upgrades over their predecessors at every working band, at lower or comparable current than before, using a band-relocation recipe (caps/resistors/bias only, inductors fixed) that is simpler to justify for a real design than retuning the inductors per band. Between the two, dev_ilir's inductive degeneration wins on raw match quality (S11/S22 routinely beyond −15 to −30dB) at lower current, while dev_ghaith's active degeneration remains the simpler topology to lay out.
