CG Cascode Exploration

Does stacking a cascode transistor on the common-gate (CG) input device help either LNA core? Tested on both main_lowtailnoise_pexmatched (CG-tank variant) and dev_ilir_pexmatched, with and without a small series peaking inductor in the cascode's drain path, at 2.4 GHz against the real PEX-extracted pad.

✕ Not adopted on either topology — net negative

↓ Jump to the follow-up optimization (sizing, peaking, bias, matching network, 15 GHz verification)

Why try it, and the design

The CG stage's cascode (M2C) sits between M2's drain and the existing drain load, gate tied directly to vdd — in this testbench VDD is an ideal DC source, i.e. already an AC ground, so this gives a clean AC-grounded cascode gate with no extra bias generator needed. Sized identically to M2 on both topologies. The motivation: reduce Miller feedback from M2's drain and improve reverse isolation (S12).

The actual result runs the other way: isolation improves a lot (S12 drops by ~25dB on dev_ilir_pexmatched) but neither design needed more isolation — both already had excellent S12 — and the cascode's own channel noise, injected directly into the signal path, costs real NF. A small series peaking inductor between the cascode's drain and the load was tried on both topologies to see if it could claw back the lost gain/bandwidth; it didn't.

Corrected: a chart bug here previously made the cascode look wideband — it isn't

This chart used to plot the + cascode series using the baseline's frequency axis, since both arrays happened to have 300 points — but the cascode had actually only been simulated over 2.04–2.76 GHz. Stretching those points across the baseline's full 200 MHz–7 GHz axis made the curve look like it held good gain and good NF from roughly 1.5–5 GHz. The cascode device has now been re-simulated over the full 1 MHz–15 GHz range (same device: W=40µm, bulk=VSS, gate=vdd, no peaking) so the chart above shows its real behavior, not a mis-scaled slice of it.

With the real data in hand: S21≥10dB does hold from 1.64–2.94 GHz (1.30 GHz, genuinely the widest gain skirt of any variant in this project) and NF does stay reasonably low across most of that span. But the actual matched band (S11 & S22 ≤ −10 dB and S21 ≥ 10 dB simultaneously) is only 2.21–2.51 GHz — 301 MHz, less than a quarter of the gain bandwidth. Outside 1–15 GHz there is no data either way. NF is computed from the real (possibly mismatched) source/load impedance at each frequency, not a re-matched one — a quiet, high-gain stage can report a flat, good-looking NF number well outside the band where it's actually power-matched. Flat gain, flat NF, and good return loss are three independent conditions, not one implying the others.

Follow-up: optimizing the cascode (main_lowtailnoise_pexmatched)

The verdict above flagged re-sizing/re-biasing M2C as the logical next step rather than peaking alone. This follow-up swept every lever independently — body tie, M2C width, a drain peaking inductor, M2C's gate bias, and the surrounding matching network — then checked whether they combine, and ran the best practical result out to 15 GHz.

1. Body effect: tie M2C's bulk to its own source?

Device: M2C, W=40µm (original sizing), gate tied to vdd, no peaking. Only the bulk node changes: VSS (the comparison baseline) vs. M2C's own source node (deep-NWELL isolated, removes body effect). 2.4 GHz point only.

GF180MCU has a real deep-NWELL option, so M2C's bulk doesn't have to sit at VSS. Tested directly in SPICE rather than assumed: removing the body effect made NF very slightly worse (4.54→4.65 dB at the original W=40µm sizing), not better — gm shifted on both M2 and M2C together through the shared bias/current path, a more tangled interaction than single-device gm alone. Not used going forward.

2. M2C sizing — a real, non-monotonic optimum near W=15µm

Device: M2C, W swept 8–80µm (16 points), bulk=VSS, gate tied to vdd, no peaking, original bias/matching. 2.4 GHz point only, per width.

Narrower M2C at the same forced bias current → higher VOV → lower gm (gm=2·ID/VOV) → less of M2C's own channel noise injected into the signal path. That holds until the device gets so narrow it stops behaving sensibly; the sweep (bulk tied to VSS) finds a genuine minimum, not a one-directional trend.

NF vs. M2C width at 2.4 GHz, 16 points from 8–80µm. Minimum at W=15–16µm (NF=4.186 dB) vs. W=40µm as originally added (NF=4.537 dB) — a 0.35 dB recovery from sizing alone, no other change.

3. Peaking inductor on top of the sizing optimum

Device: M2C, W=15µm (the sizing optimum above), bulk=VSS, gate tied to vdd, original bias/matching. A series inductor LPEAK (0–20 nH swept) inserted between M2C's drain and the existing LD1/RLD1 load node — once as an ideal element, once with a series R=ωL/12 (Q=12 convention) added for realism. 2.4 GHz point only, per L.

Unlike peaking on the original W=40µm sizing (barely moved NF, see above), peaking on top of the W=15µm optimum keeps improving NF with no sign of a plateau — checked both with an ideal lossless inductor and with the project's standard Q=12 coil-loss convention (R=ωL/Q) applied in series. The realistic-loss case tracks the ideal case closely, confirming this isn't just an artifact of an unphysically perfect inductor.

ideal LQ=12 lossy L

Trend is genuine but a 13–20 nH on-chip spiral is a large, area-hungry structure whose real self-resonant frequency (not modeled here) would likely intrude well before 2.4 GHz — treat the far end of this curve as a model-validity caveat, not a free lunch. A practical peaking value in the 3–6 nH range is a safer recommendation: NF 4.19→4.01–3.86 dB with full S11/S22 margin preserved (≤−22 dB).

4 & 5. M2C gate bias, and matching-network retune — smaller levers, and not additive with sizing+peaking

Bias sweep device: M2C, W=40µm (original sizing, not the W=15 optimum), bulk=VSS, gate decoupled from vdd onto its own node and swept VBIAS_M2C∈{2.0,2.5,3.0,3.5,4.0,4.5,5.0(=control/vdd-tied)} V, VBIAS_CG=1.40 V fixed, no peaking.
Matchnet-retune device: same M2C, W=40µm, bulk=VSS, gate=vdd (untouched), no peaking — only the surrounding network changes: LIN 4.2→3.2 nH (with matching Q=12 series R) and C5 15.13→10µm, found via a 155-point sweep over LIN, C2, C5.
“All four levers” row below: M2C W=15µm + 6 nH Q=12 peaking + VBIAS_M2C=3.2 V + LIN=3.2 nH/C5=10µm, all stacked together.

Each works on its own: sweeping M2C's gate bias (decoupled from vdd) at the original W=40µm sizing finds a shallow optimum around VBIAS_M2C≈3.0–3.5 V (NF 4.54→4.40 dB, a 0.13 dB win). Independently, retuning the input matching network (LIN 4.2→3.2 nH, C5 15.13→10µm) at the original sizing recovers NF to 4.31 dB — but by spending S11 margin the design never needed (S11 −28→−12 dB).

Naively stacking all four levers (W=15, +6 nH peaking, VBIAS_M2C=3.2, retuned LIN/C5) gives NF=3.854 dB — essentially identical to sizing+peaking alone (3.86 dB) — while S11 degrades to −12.5 dB. The bias and matchnet levers are mostly reaching for the same noise-recovery headroom that sizing+peaking already captures; applying them on top doesn't add NF improvement, it just spends match margin for nothing. The practical recommendation is sizing+peaking only, with bias and matching left at their original values.

Net result of this whole follow-up: the untuned cascode's NF penalty vs. the no-cascode baseline shrinks from 1.59 dB (2.95→4.54) to 0.91 dB (2.95→3.86) — a real, substantial, well-characterized improvement — but the gap is not closed, and the design is just as narrowband-matched as every other variant in this project despite its deceptively wide gain skirt. The cascode remains not recommended for adoption; if ever revisited, W=15µm + 3–6 nH peaking is the configuration to start from.

Verdict

Not recommended on either topology, as implemented

Both LNA cores are noise-cancelling CG+CS designs where NF is a primary figure of merit. The cascode trades real NF (+0.86 to +1.6dB) and some gain for a large S12/isolation improvement that neither design needed — both already had S12 well below what any practical spec would require. A series peaking inductor in the cascode's drain path does not recover the loss on either core; it shifts the gain peak away from the design frequency instead of extending it.

  • main_lowtailnoise_pexmatched: NF 2.95→4.54dB, S21 28.6→25.2dB. Not committed — schematic reverted to the LD1/RLD1-only state.
  • dev_ilir_pexmatched: NF 2.49→3.35dB, S21 17.97→16.34dB. Committed as a clearly-labeled, honest comparison record (not adopted as the design's working state).

Update from the follow-up above: re-sizing M2C (W=40→15µm) plus a practical 3–6 nH drain peaking inductor recovers most of that gap on main_lowtailnoise_pexmatched — NF penalty shrinks from 1.59 dB to 0.86–0.91 dB, a real and substantial win, with full S11/S22 margin preserved. Gate-bias tuning and matching-network retuning each help in isolation but don't stack on top of sizing+peaking (same noise-recovery headroom, reached a different way), and a full 1 MHz–15 GHz check confirms the optimized design is still narrowband-matched (267 MHz) despite its much wider gain skirt (1.47 GHz). The remaining ~0.9 dB gap to the no-cascode baseline is not closed by any lever tried — the cascode stays not recommended for adoption, but is now a well-characterized, near-optimal design point should isolation ever become the binding constraint.
